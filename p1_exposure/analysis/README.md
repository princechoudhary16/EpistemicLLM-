# P1 exposure analysis

How does the number of times a fact was shown in fine-tuning change what the model does with it?
This folder holds the analysis code and its results for the synthetic exposure study (plan.md,
P1, Weeks 3 and 4). It reads response files in the shared schema and writes only to its own
`results/` folder.

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `exposure_analysis.py` | Tables, CSVs and figures: accuracy, hallucination, abstention, coverage, selective accuracy, mean K and K buckets by exposure level, with fact-level bootstrap intervals; splits by relation and by dev/final wording; the 0x chance floor; model confidence |
| `robustness_report.py` | Week 4: training-seed spread, a second synthetic world, and paired held-out/swapped wording comparisons |
| `results/` | Committed outputs. Every table is regenerated by the commands below; each output folder records the sha256 of its inputs |

```bash
python -m p1_exposure.analysis.exposure_analysis --version main_v0 --primary sft_matched \
    --responses sft_matched=results/raw/p1_main_v0_sft_matched.jsonl base=results/raw/p1_main_v0_base.jsonl \
    --out-dir p1_exposure/analysis/results/main_v0
python -m unittest discover -s p1_exposure/tests -t .
```

Every generated report starts with a definitions block (exposure, greedy, K, hallucination, coverage,
selective accuracy, the bootstrap). Uncertainty resamples whole facts within each exposure level
because the two questions about a fact are not independent.

## Results: `main_v0`, standard SFT with the matched recipe (`sft_matched`)

Greedy accuracy by exposure, 200 questions (100 facts) per level. Full tables, figures and the
by-relation, dev/final and confidence breakdowns are in `results/main_v0/exposure_analysis.md`.

| Exposure | Accuracy [95% CI] | Mean K | Facts K=0 / inconsistent / K>=0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x | 0.0% [0.0, 0.0] | 0.00 | 99 / 1 / 0 |
| 1x | 5.0% [1.5, 9.0] | 0.03 | 92 / 6 / 2 |
| 2x | 26.5% [18.5, 34.5] | 0.22 | 64 / 20 / 16 |
| 5x | 71.5% [63.0, 79.5] | 0.61 | 21 / 27 / 52 |
| 10x | 87.5% [82.0, 93.0] | 0.79 | 8 / 20 / 72 |
| 20x | 90.5% [85.0, 95.0] | 0.88 | 2 / 15 / 83 |

1. **Learning is gradual, not a threshold at one exposure.** Accuracy is already above zero at 1x
   (5.0%), rises steepest between 2x and 5x (+45.0 points, CI +33.5 to +56.0), and flattens: 10x to
   20x adds 3.0 points with an interval that includes zero (-4.5 to +10.0).
2. **The model never abstains** (coverage is 100% at every level), so for this model hallucination is
   simply 1 minus accuracy: 100% at 0x falling to 9.5% at 20x. Any hallucination figure from this
   study must be read together with coverage.
3. **Relations differ.** `birth_year` is the slowest learner (35% at 5x, 85% at 20x) against 70-85% at
   5x and 85-100% at 20x for the other four relations. Exposure is balanced across relations by
   construction (20 facts per relation per level), so this is a difficulty offset, not a confound.
4. **Dev and final facts behave alike** at every level (confidence intervals overlap throughout), so
   neither the fact subset nor the validation-versus-test wording moves the curve by more than the
   interval width. The held-out paraphrase run separates the two directly.
5. **Chance floor is near zero but not structurally zero.** 2 of 1,200 sampled responses at 0x were
   correct, both `cartographer`. At 0x the model's guesses for `occupation` concentrate on two answers
   (`tapestry weaver` 72 of 200 samples, `cooper` 71), so the floor for that relation depends on how many
   of a world's 0x facts happen to have those answers.
6. **Confidence tracks correctness but wrong answers do not become unconfident.** Mean log-probability of
   a correct greedy answer rises from -0.40 at 1x to -0.06 at 20x; incorrect answers stay near -0.6 until
   10x and are still -0.39 at 20x.

## Caveats

- Grading is P6's placeholder substring grader (`placeholder_exact_v1`). Regrading with P2's shared
  whole-word grader (v3) changes 0.07-0.19% of rows and moves accuracy by at most 0.1 points here.
- One synthetic world and one training seed in this table; the robustness report covers both.
- `base` and `sft_weak_recipe` rows come from P6's original runs under `decoding_v0`, which kept
  Qwen's default `repetition_penalty` of 1.1. They are superseded for the final comparison.
- Mean K and K buckets use only the 5 sampled responses per question, so each fact's K rests on 10 samples.
